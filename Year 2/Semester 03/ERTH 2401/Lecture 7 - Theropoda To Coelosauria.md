# 1. Dinosauria Overview

## Two Main Dinosaur Groups

### Ornithischia

- “Bird-hipped” dinosaurs
    
- Mostly herbivorous
    

### Saurischia

- “Lizard-hipped” dinosaurs
    
- Includes:
    
    - Sauropodomorpha
        
    - Theropoda
        

## Key Saurischian Traits

- Specialized pelvis structure
    
- Long neck vertebrae in many members
    
- Large hands and grasping ability
    
- Loss of carpal 5 in wrist
    
- Slightly twisted first digit for grasping
    

---

# 2. Theropoda

## Definition

- Theropods are all taxa sharing a more recent common ancestor with birds than with sauropodomorphs.
    

## Meaning of the Name

- “Theropoda” = “Beast Foot”
    

## General Characteristics

- Mostly bipedal
    
- Narrow-bodied
    
- Clawed fingers and toes
    
- Serrated teeth
    
- Flattened teeth for cutting flesh
    
- Digitigrade posture (walk on toes)
    
- Constant tooth replacement
    

## Size Range

- Included both:
    
    - Small forms (ex: Microraptor)
        
    - Giant predators (ex: Tyrannosaurus rex)
        

---

# 3. Important Theropod Features

## Teeth

- Teeth are laterally compressed and serrated.
    
- Adapted for slicing meat.
    
- Teeth were replaced continuously throughout life.
    

## Pleurocoels

- Hollow openings in vertebrae.
    
- Reduced body weight.
    
- Related to advanced breathing systems.
    

## Body Design

- Lightweight skeletons.
    
- Efficient movement and running ability.
    
- Strong hind limbs.
    

---

# 4. Theropod Locomotion

## Digitigrade Posture

- Theropods walked on their toes.
    
- Increased stride length and speed.
    

## Metatarsals

- Foot bones overlap tightly.
    
- Improved strength and efficiency.
    

## Running Adaptations

- Efficient locomotion.
    
- Long strides.
    
- Energetically efficient movement.
    

---

# 5. Theropod Hands and Feet

## Digits

- Most theropods had digits 1, 2, and 3.
    
- Digits 4 and 5 were reduced or lost in advanced forms.
    
- Tyrannosaurus rex had only two functional fingers.
    

## Claws

- Fingers and toes ended in claws.
    
- Used for grasping prey.
    

## Killing Claw

- Enlarged second toe claw in raptorial theropods.
    
- Seen in animals like Velociraptor.
    
- Used for attacking prey.
    

## First Digit

- Slightly twisted.
    
- Helped with grasping.
    
- Comparable to grasping adaptations in birds.
    

---

# 6. Theropod Skull and Feeding

## Skull Diversity

- Theropod skulls varied widely in shape.
    
- Different skull shapes reflected different feeding strategies.
    

## Feeding Adaptations

- Most theropods were carnivorous.
    
- Strong jaw muscles generated powerful bite forces.
    
- Shorter, deeper skulls usually had stronger bites.
    

## Feeding Style

- Theropods did not chew food.
    
- Teeth sliced flesh into swallowable pieces.
    

## Gastroliths

- Some theropods swallowed stones.
    
- Stones may have helped process food in the stomach.
    

---

# 7. Theropods and Birds

## Evolutionary Relationship

- Birds are living theropod dinosaurs.
    

## Maniraptorans

- Group of theropods closely related to birds.
    
- Forelimbs became elongated over time.
    
- Eventually evolved into wings.
    

## Microraptor

- Small feathered theropod.
    
- May have been capable of gliding or flight.
    

---

# 8. Tyrannosaurus rex Notes

## Key Ideas Mentioned

- One of the largest carnivorous theropods.
    
- Over 30 relatively complete skeletons known.
    
- Reduced forelimbs.
    
- Massive skull and bite force.
    

## Functional Interpretation

- T. rex primarily interacted with the environment using its head and jaws rather than its arms.
    

---

# 9. Dinosaur Growth

## Indeterminate Growth

- Dinosaurs likely continued growing throughout much of life.
    
- Largest known specimens may not represent maximum possible size.
    

## Harsh Life Conditions

- Injuries and disease likely prevented many dinosaurs from reaching old age.

# Big Picture Summary

- Theropods were primarily bipedal carnivorous dinosaurs.
    
- They evolved specialized feet, claws, teeth, and lightweight skeletons for active predation.
    
- Advanced theropods developed traits that eventually led to birds.
    
- Birds are modern living theropod dinosaurs.
    
- Theropod diversity ranged from tiny feathered forms to giant apex predators like T. rex.
